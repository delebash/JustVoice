What these columns mean

Book says Lines whose speaker the book names right next to them — “said Marius” — or earlier in the same paragraph. Most speech has no name beside it: “she said” and a gesture before the line name nobody.
AI decided Lines whose speaker the AI worked out from the story around them. Most spoken lines are decided this way, and the AI is right on nearly all of them — but it can be sure and wrong, so its confidence alone is not a warning.
Flagged Lines where the AI most often goes wrong, so you know where to read closely: one person speaking three times with no reply, a persona's only line in the chapter, dialogue given to the Narrator, the book and the AI naming different speakers. These are the lines worth your eyes.
No speaker The AI wasn't sure, or gave no answer. These block rendering.
from the import The script named its speakers, so there was nothing to analyze. The checks above run only on what Analyze decided.
Renn added since Analyzed before that persona joined the cast, and the text names them. Re-analyze it — lines you set are kept.
no dialogue found Nothing in the text was read as speech. Speech in ‘single quotes’, «guillemets» or after a dash isn't read as dialogue, so every line went to the Narrator.
can't re-cut The text changed after takes were recorded. Analyzing would re-cut the lines and delete those takes, so it stops.
failed The AI call failed. The reason is on the row, and nothing was saved.

What the columns mean

Speaker Who says the line. Change it here; a line you set is yours, and Analyze leaves it alone.
Decided by Why the line has its speaker. Narration — text outside quote marks, read by the Narrator. Words in quotes, like “said Marius” — the book names the speaker right there; you can check it against the text. AI, from the story around it — nothing names the speaker, so the AI worked it out; most spoken lines are decided this way. You — you set or confirmed it.
Confidence How sure the AI said it was. A low number is worth a look. A high one is not proof — the AI has been 100% sure and wrong.
Check Where the AI most often goes wrong, so you know where to read closely. Its most common mistake is losing track of turns in a back-and-forth — two people alternate, and it gives two lines in a row to one of them. So a line is marked when one person speaks three times with no reply, when it is a persona's only line in the chapter, when dialogue went to the Narrator, or when the book and the AI name different speakers. The line may well be right: “👁 Show the lines around” lets you read the exchange, and “✓ Looks right” (optional) removes the mark — the line renders the same either way.

Direction, takes and rendering are not on this page. Script decides who says what; how it is performed is Render.

Speech enginesVRAM 3.2 / 8.6 GB Kokoro
One speech engine is resident at a time. Loading another unloads the current one — that's why auditioning across engines costs a swap, and why every listen button in the app warns before it charges you a minute.

Picking an engine is picking what a voice can do

You choose an engine once, when you cast a persona to a voice. From then on it decides three things for every line that persona speaks: whether you can clone their timbre, whether they can be directed in words, and whether a per-line emotion reaches the model. Nothing else in the app can put back what the engine doesn't do.

Someone's own voice, and per-line emotion Chatterbox Turbo — cloning Written direction is ignored. English only.
Someone's own voice, in another language Chatterbox Multilingual — 23 languages No direction and no emotion — timbre and the numbers.
